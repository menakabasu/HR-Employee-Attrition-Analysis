
# HR Employee Attrition Analysis

## 📊 Project Overview

This project analyses employee attrition using **SQL Server and Power BI** to identify key factors associated with employee turnover and highlight areas where HR teams can focus retention efforts.

The project covers employee demographics, departments, job roles, overtime, satisfaction, work-life balance, income, tenure and employee risk segmentation.

The analysis moves from **data exploration and SQL analysis** to an interactive **Power BI dashboard** and business recommendations.

---

## 🎯 Business Objectives

The main objectives of this project are to:

* Measure the overall employee attrition rate.
* Identify departments with higher employee attrition.
* Identify job roles with higher attrition.
* Analyse attrition by age group and gender.
* Investigate the relationship between overtime and attrition.
* Analyse job satisfaction, environment satisfaction and work-life balance.
* Investigate attrition across income categories and tenure bands.
* Identify high-risk employee segments.
* Analyse employees earning below their department average salary.
* Provide data-driven recommendations for employee retention.

## 💡 Key Findings

### Employee Attrition

The overall employee attrition rate is **16.14%**, with 237 employees leaving out of 1,468 employees.

### Department

**Sales** has the highest department attrition rate at approximately **20.7%**.

### Job Role

**Sales Representative** has the highest attrition rate at approximately **39.8%**.

### Overtime

Employees working overtime have a substantially higher attrition rate, reaching approximately **30.5%**.

### Age

The **18–25 age group** has the highest attrition rate among the analysed age groups.

### Tenure

Employees with **0–2 years of tenure** show the highest attrition rate at approximately **29.82%**.

### Income

The lowest income category has the highest attrition rate at approximately **21.79%**, while the very-high-income category has the lowest rate at approximately **3.76%**.

### Employee Risk

The analysis identified **140 high-risk employees**, representing approximately **9.54%** of employees.

### Salary

Approximately **67.1% of employees earn below their department's average salary**, highlighting an area for further compensation analysis.

## 📌 Business Recommendations

Based on the analysis:

1. **Review sales retention strategies**
   Investigate sales targets, incentives, workload and career progression.

2. **Monitor overtime**
   Review overtime patterns and workload distribution to reduce potential burnout.

3. **Strengthen new-employee onboarding**
   Introduce structured onboarding, mentoring and 30/60/90-day reviews.

4. **Improve employee satisfaction**
   Monitor employees with consistently low satisfaction scores and investigate underlying causes.

5. **Review compensation**
   Compare employee salaries against departmental averages and review progression opportunities.

6. **Monitor high-risk employees**
   Establish a regular review process for employees with multiple risk factors.

7. **Conduct role-specific retention analysis**
   Investigate job roles with unusually high attrition and identify the underlying causes.
